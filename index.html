Inverting Speaker Characteristics via Model Inversion

George Brown

Abstract

Model inversion (MI) attacks reconstruct the training data from a trained machine learning model, but their application to speaker recognition remains underexplored, with prior work evaluating success only within the victim's own representation and unable to produce speaker characteristics corresponding with the individuals in the original dataset. We propose an MI attack that recovers the training voices from a speaker recognition model by optimising the speaker conditioning d-vector of a frozen zero-shot TTS system end-to-end through the frozen victim. We demonstrate using an independent evaluation model that our reconstructed voices contain speaker identity, gender and accent information. Across SincNet, x-vector and ECAPA-TDNN victims fine-tuned on 30 VCTK speakers, our attacks achieve an MI accuracy of 98–100% within the victim's own embedding space, recover identity in the independent space at 23–63%, recover gender at 63–97%, and accent at 37–74%, with each result performing significantly above chance. Our results show that speaker identity information can be leaked via MI attacks on speaker recognition models.
